Reading the confusion matrix — there's an interesting asymmetry,
opposite in direction from what we saw in Q5:

-   topk: precision 1.00, recall 0.93 — when the model predicts topk,
    it's always right (1,176/1,176). It misses 90 real topk submissions,
    predicting bubblesort instead.

-   bubblesort: precision 0.69, recall 0.98 — when a submission is
    genuinely bubblesort, the model almost always catches it (200/204).
    But it's a "greedy" label — 90 real topk submissions get swept into
    it too, dragging precision down.

So bubblesort is acting like the "broad, easy-to-fall-into" cluster, and
topk is the "narrow, only-matched-when-truly-similar" cluster. That's
the reverse of Q5, where builtin (analogous role) was the tight cluster
and manual was the broad one. Makes some sense structurally: real
bubble-sort implementations are fairly homogeneous (nested loops, a
comparison, a swap — not much room for variation), while the "find max,
remove, append" topk strategy admits more surface variety (different
ways to find the max, different removal idioms) — so a topk submission
that happens to look a bit unusual can drift close enough to the tighter
bubblesort cluster to get misclassified there.

For the paper, if this goes in: this is actually a nicer story than Q5's
— a higher, tighter number, plus a genuinely informative asymmetry that
runs the opposite direction, which itself makes for a good comparative
point ("cluster tightness, not correctness, drives asymmetric
misclassification — and which strategy is the 'tight' one isn't fixed,
it depends on how much real-world implementation variety that specific
strategy actually admits").

The 95.9% accuracy is almost entirely a class-imbalance artifact, not a
real signal. counter is 96.8% of the pooled test set (1,694/1,750). A
model that always predicted counter, regardless of input, would score
~96.8% accuracy — actually higher than what this classifier achieved. So
"96% accuracy" here doesn't mean the model recovered the taxonomic
split; it means the majority class dominates the metric.

The number that actually matters is functional's own precision/recall —
and it's close to uninformative:

-   Precision 0.33, recall 0.27 — the model correctly identifies only
