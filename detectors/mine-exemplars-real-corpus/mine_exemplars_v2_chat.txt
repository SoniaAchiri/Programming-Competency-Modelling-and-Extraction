Broadly yes, but let me be precise about what kind of limitation, since
"embedding similarity is bad" would overclaim it.

It's not that embedding similarity is unreliable in general — the whole
validation (88–91% pairwise win rate, 74–82% worst-case) shows it does
separate related from unrelated code well on average. The specific
limitation is narrower: embeddings tend to capture holistic/topical code
shape, not the presence or absence of one small, semantically pivotal
detail — here, a single function call that happens to be the entire
definition of correctness. Two snippets with nearly identical loop
shape, condition structure, and return pattern will embed as very
similar even when the one token sequence that makes them structurally
different (and unique_day(...)) is entirely missing from one of them.
The embedding is answering "does this look like the same kind of
function," not "does this satisfy the specific defining criterion."

This is a known category of weakness in embedding models generally, not
something specific to codet5p or a bug in the scope-fix — it's the
code-similarity analogue of sentence embeddings rating "the cat sat on
the mat" and "the cat did not sit on the mat" as highly similar despite
the negation flipping the meaning.

Worth noting: this is actually a vindication of the project's existing
design, not a new problem it exposes. It's exactly why semantic_backup
is built as advisory-only — flagged for manual review, never
auto-credited into final_score. This result is a concrete example of why
that guardrail matters.
